Estimated climate response surfaces from the paper A Neural Network Approach to the Climate-Growth Relationship. Each surface is the architecture selected by the Bayesian information criterion (a single hidden layer with two neurons), fitted with country and time fixed effects and country-specific trends concentrated out. Surfaces show the climate contribution to growth, centred at the sample-mean climate. Drag to rotate and scroll to zoom.

Global model

BIC-selected network. The response is approximately linear at low temperatures and turns concave as temperature rises, with a higher growth-maximizing temperature than the quadratic benchmark.

Regional model: Europe

Approximately linear, weakly positive temperature response; little role for precipitation.

Regional model: Africa

Negative temperature response under dry conditions, mitigated at higher precipitation.

Regional model: Asia

Concave response: moderate warming helps cooler economies, while further warming reduces growth.

Regional model: Oceania

Broadly positive but imprecisely estimated — few countries and low data support, so interpret with caution.

Regional model: Americas

Concave response peaking near 14 °C; the region spans a wide climate range, so the extremes are sparsely supported.

Income group: Low-income

Sample split at the median of GDP per capita. Growth falls under hot, dry conditions; higher precipitation partly offsets the effect.

Income group: High-income

Nearly flat across the climate space — richer economies appear less sensitive to the temperature and precipitation variation observed in the sample.
